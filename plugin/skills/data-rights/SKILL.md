## Step 1: Data inventory

For each source, record:

| Source | How obtained | Contains personal data? | Contains third-party IP? | Planned uses (train / fine-tune / RAG / eval / output) |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Typical sources: customer-uploaded content, product usage and feedback logs, public web scraping, licensed or purchased datasets, open datasets, synthetic data generated by other models, user faces/voices, partner data.

## Step 2: Rights check per source

For each source and use, assess:
- **Contract & terms**: customer agreements, DPA, terms of service, dataset licenses (commercial use? derivatives? attribution?), website terms and robots/opt-out signals for scraped data
- **Model provider terms**: whether outputs of their models may be used to train competing models
- **Privacy law**: lawful basis (e.g. GDPR consent / legitimate interest), purpose limitation, data minimization, special-category data (health, biometrics, children's data), cross-border transfer
- **Copyright & IP**: training vs output risks; memorization and regurgitation; licensing options
- **Likeness & biometrics**: consent for faces and voices; biometric privacy laws
- **Sector rules**: health (e.g. HIPAA), finance, education (e.g. FERPA), as applicable

Rate each: 🟢 clear rights / 🟡 needs action / 🔴 do not use until resolved.

## Step 3: Required actions

- Contract changes (customer terms allowing product improvement, opt-outs, enterprise no-training defaults)
- Consent flows and notices
- Technical controls: PII scrubbing, de-identification, tenant isolation, deletion pipelines, opt-out handling, output filters against regurgitation
- Licensing deals worth pursuing for high-value data

## Step 4: Documentation

What to keep for diligence and audits: data inventory, licenses, consent records, data lineage per model version, deletion logs, datasheets for training sets.
